Track record

—

A true chronological walk-forward backtest of every one of the 1,312 games in the 2025–26 NHL regular season, graded against real closing totals and odds. Each eligible game (both teams with 10+ games played) re-runs the model using only what was known before that night: each team's offense weighted against the specific opponent's defense, recency-weighted (15-game half-life) and blended toward the league average for small samples. Early-season games before a team hit 10 GP are marked not enough data; toss-ups (model never got past 60% either way) and pushes (final total matched the line exactly) are shown but excluded from the Record/Hit rate/Profit/ROI tiles below, same as a real sportsbook would void those bets.

How solid is this? Honestly — promising, not proven. The record below is 196 graded bets, and a 55.6% hit rate over 196 picks is about 1.6 standard deviations above a coin flip — roughly a 1-in-8 chance a result at least this good happens by luck even with zero real edge. What makes it more credible than a single lucky number: splitting the season in half and checking each independently, both halves show a positive edge on their own (+4.36% ROI first half, +3.37% second half), while the same model at a lower 52% confidence bar is negative in both halves — consistent with every other version of this model tested. That consistency is reassuring. It isn't proof.
