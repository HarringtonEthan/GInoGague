The math, in plain terms

Poisson distribution

Goals don't follow a bell curve — they're rare, semi-random events clustered around an average. The Poisson distribution is the standard way statisticians model exactly that, and it's the math behind every number above.

The projection

λ (lambda) isn't just two scoring averages added together. Each team's offense is weighted against the specific opponent's defense, not the league as a whole, and normalized by the league's overall scoring rate. Recent games count more than early-season ones (a 15-game half-life), and a team with only a handful of games on the board gets blended toward the league average so a hot or cold week doesn't overstate what's actually known yet.

The edge

Sportsbook odds imply their own probability. Edge is the model's probability minus the market's. Positive means the model sees more value than the price suggests — negative means the market's already ahead of it. Only picks at 60%+ confidence are treated as real bets; everything else is a toss-up.
