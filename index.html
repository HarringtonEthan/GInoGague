The math, in plain terms

Poisson general formula

Goals don't follow a bell curve — they're rare, semi-random events clustered around an average. The Poisson distribution is the standard way statisticians model exactly that: hand it a projected average and it converts that into a probability for every possible final total. That's the general-purpose formula — it doesn't know or care where the average came from.

Our formula

One formula, used everywhere on this site — the "Build the matchup" tool above, Opening Night, and the Experimental Model's backtests all calculate the projected average the same way. Worth knowing: this formula's settings (how much recent games matter, how hard small samples get reined in, the confidence bar) were chosen by fitting to the known 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons, not decided blind beforehand — see the Experimental Model page for what that means and why the live 2026-27 season is the real test of whether it holds up.

The edge

Sportsbook odds imply their own probability. Edge is the model's probability minus the market's. Positive means the model sees more value than the price suggests — negative means the market's already ahead of it. Only picks at 58%+ confidence are treated as real bets; everything else is a toss-up.

Poisson general formula
P(goals = k) = (e^−projected total × projected totalᵏ) ÷ k!
Our formula
projected total = (home GF × away GA + away GF × home GA) ÷ league average

What the variables mean

k
One specific possible combined-goals total for the game. Plug in any k and the Poisson formula gives you the probability of the game landing on exactly that total.
projected total
The model's single best estimate of combined goals for this matchup — what "our formula" solves for, and what then gets fed into the Poisson formula above to produce Over/Under percentages.
home GF, away GF
That team's goals-for, recency-weighted (a game from three weeks ago counts half as much as last night's) and blended toward the league average so a hot or cold streak in a small sample doesn't get overweighted.
home GA, away GA
The same idea, for goals-against.
league average
The league-wide average goals scored per team per game, over the same season used to weight GF and GA above.
