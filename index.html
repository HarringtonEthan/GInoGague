The math, in plain terms

Poisson distribution

Goals don't follow a bell curve — they're rare, semi-random events clustered around an average. The Poisson distribution is the standard way statisticians model exactly that. Once you have a projected combined-goals average (λ) for a matchup, the Poisson formula converts it into a full probability for every possible total — which is what turns into an Over/Under percentage. This is the math behind both models below.

The Track Record model

The Track Record page grades a genuine walk-forward model: for every 2025-26 game, it only uses each team's actual results from games played strictly before that night (a team needs 10+ games played before it's even eligible). Each team's goals-for and goals-against are recency-weighted — a game from 3 weeks ago counts half as much as last night's — and blended toward the league average so a hot or cold streak in a small sample doesn't get overweighted. λ then pairs each team's offense against the specific opponent's defense, both directions, scaled by the league-wide scoring rate at that point in the season. This is what actually produced the 109-87 (55.6%), +$751, +3.8% ROI record over 196 graded games.

The live matchup model

The "Build the matchup" tool above and the Opening Night projections use a different, forward-looking model: every team gets an attack rating and a defense rating fit simultaneously across the complete, finished 2025-26 season, chosen so the model's expected goals match what actually happened league-wide, all at once. That's legitimate for projecting a genuinely future game (like 2026-27) — but it's not the model behind the Track Record numbers, since it was fit with knowledge of the whole season's results. The two are kept deliberately separate.

The edge

Sportsbook odds imply their own probability. Edge is the model's probability minus the market's. Positive means the model sees more value than the price suggests — negative means the market's already ahead of it. Only picks at 60%+ confidence are treated as real bets; everything else is a toss-up.
